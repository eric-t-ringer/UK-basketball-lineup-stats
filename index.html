    Usage vs. results

    Each dot is a five-man lineup. Further right means more minutes; higher means a better adjusted scoring margin per 40 minutes. Short stints sit near the team average until they've played enough to say otherwise. Lineups below the minimum-minutes setting show as hollow circles.

    Player on/off

    Scoring margin per 40 minutes with each player on the floor versus off it. On/off is noisy over few games, and it credits players for who they share the floor with.

    All lineups

    Player combinations

    How the numbers work

    Source: the "Line Up Analysis" page of Kentucky's official box score book for each game, posted on UK Athletics. Each row there is a five-man unit's time on the floor, points for and against, and Kentucky's points per possession.

    Possessions are worked out as points ÷ points per possession, from the box score. For units that didn't score, possessions are estimated from their minutes at that game's pace. OER and DER are points scored and allowed per 100 possessions, using Kentucky's possessions for both ends (over a stretch of play the two teams' possession counts differ by at most one). They're close approximations, not official figures.

    How the measures relate: OER − DER is the scoring margin per 100 possessions. Net /40 is the same margin per 40 minutes, so it also reflects pace: at Kentucky’s exhibition pace of about 69 possessions per 40 minutes, a +10 margin per 100 possessions is about +7 per 40. Adj. /40 is Net /40 pulled toward the team average for small samples; OER and DER are shown unadjusted, so treat them with the same caution on short stints.

    Exhibitions phase out: the main view includes the three exhibition games until there are three official games. From then on, each new official game replaces the oldest exhibition (the 4th official game replaces Little Rock, the 5th Texas Tech, the 6th UCF), so after six official games the main view is official games only. The exhibitions stay viewable under the "Exhibitions" choice in the Games filter.

    Adjusted net (Adj. /40): a lineup that played 2 minutes can show +60 per 40 on one run, so raw margins over short stints are mostly noise. The adjusted figure blends each lineup's actual margin with the team's average margin, as if the lineup had also played 50 extra minutes at the team's average. A lineup that has played a few minutes stays close to the team average; one that has played 100+ minutes is judged mostly on its own results. The 50-minute weight is a judgment call, not a fitted value. Rankings and the chart use the adjusted figure. The idea follows Evan Miya's point that observed lineup results should count for more as a lineup logs more possessions.

    Notable dates: a big game is a triple-double, 30+ points, 6+ made threes, or 80%+ shooting on at least 10 shots. Streaks are 3+ straight official games with 20+ points or a double-double, and 5+ straight official wins. A starting-lineup change compares each game's starters with the game before.

    Combinations: pair and trio numbers pool every stint those players shared, so they gather minutes faster than full lineups. They also credit or blame the players for whoever else was on the floor.
